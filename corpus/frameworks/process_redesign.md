## Two paths for AI

**Option A — Automate / substitute.** AI copies what people already do and takes over those tasks. Machines become substitutes for labor. Productivity can rise, but bargaining power and gains tend to concentrate with those who own the systems.

**Option B — Augment / complement.** AI enables people to do things they could not do before. Humans and machines are complements. People stay central to value creation. Augmentation also tends to create new products, services, and tasks — not only cheaper versions of old ones.

This brief prefers Option B when proposing speculative process redesign.

## What the redesign sources agree on

1. **Tech-forward fails.** Putting AI on the current job/process often yields a faster version of waste.
2. **Work-backward wins.** Deconstruct tasks → eliminate or relocate what should not exist → then automate or augment what remains; name one owner for the end-to-end result.
3. **Sequence matters.** Purpose before analysis; eliminate before automate.
4. **People redesign with AI.** Front-line staff should shape the new flow, not only IT pilots.
5. **Value is economic.** Gains count when cost, throughput, quality, or capacity for higher-value work move.

## Proposal: process redesign with AI

**Pattern we use in every brief**

| Step | Question | AI role | Human role |
|------|----------|---------|------------|
| 1. Purpose | What business outcome is this step for? | — | Name the lever (volume, price, unit cost, risk) |
| 2. Eliminate | What can disappear? | Flag duplicate/low-value steps from logs or samples | Cut re-entry, status theatre, unused approvals |
| 3. Standardize | One path in? | Enforce fields / classify intake | One exception owner |
| 4. Redesign flow | What is the new happy path? | Draft, retrieve, propose | Decide, commit money/customer/legal outcomes |
| 5. Pilot | Did a metric move? | Run on one queue/team | Baseline → compare → scale or stop |

**Business-value test.** Ship the redesign only if a named owner can point to a unit-economics lever and a baseline number to improve in the first meeting.
